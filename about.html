About the site

Why I built Family Meal Planning Tools

I’m a husband and dad, and I built this site because feeding a family is not just about cooking. Somebody has to figure out what everyone is eating, what is already in the kitchen, what needs to be bought, how much to buy, and how to make it all fit the family budget.

Meal planning is part of sharing the load

In a busy household, balance matters. Being a husband and dad means being part of the everyday responsibilities that keep the family moving—not just the big things, but the repetitive jobs that show up every single week. Meal planning, grocery shopping, figuring out dinner, and making sure there is actually enough food for everyone are part of that.

I wanted a simpler way to take ownership of that part of family life. Instead of asking the same question every afternoon—“What are we doing for dinner?”—I wanted a tool that could help turn a family size and a budget into an actual plan.

For the dads and husbands: knowing how to grill one impressive steak does not automatically count as carrying the dinner-planning department for the other 364 days of the year. This site is here to help.

Why I made the tools

The goal of Family Meal Planning Tools is to remove as much friction as possible. You can generate a weekly meal plan based on your family size and budget, get suggested ingredient quantities, build a grocery list automatically, save or print the plan, and scale recipes for the number of people you are actually feeding.

I built it for myself first, but I also wanted to share it with other dads and husbands who want to be more involved without spending an hour making spreadsheets or doing grocery math.

And yes, wives can absolutely use it too. I am not going to stop you. But if your husband keeps asking what is for dinner while holding a perfectly functional phone, sending him this site is also an option.

Use the recipe blog for ideas

If the planner gives you a blank night—or you just want something different—check out The Man That Cooks Sometimes. The recipe blog focuses on approachable family meals, practical ingredients, and food that a busy husband or dad can realistically make after a normal day.

The two sites are meant to work together: find a meal idea on the recipe blog, then bring it back here to build the week around it.

Scale my recipes for your family

The Recipe Ingredient Scaler is especially useful with recipes from The Man That Cooks Sometimes. Paste the recipe URL into the scaler, choose how many people you are feeding, and the tool will pull the ingredient list and adjust measurable quantities for your family size.

From there, you can add the scaled recipe directly to your weekly meal planner so those ingredients flow into the grocery list. That means the workflow can be as simple as: find a recipe → scale it → add it to the week → shop the list.

The whole point: less mental back-and-forth about dinner, fewer forgotten grocery items, and an easier way for both partners to share the work that keeps a family fed.

Built for real life, not perfect meal-prep people

This site is not about turning every Sunday into a four-hour meal-prep marathon. Some nights will be leftovers. Some nights will be tacos. Some nights you will decide cereal technically qualifies as dinner. The goal is not perfection—it is having enough of a plan that dinner stops becoming a daily emergency.
